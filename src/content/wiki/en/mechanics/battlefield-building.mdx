The battlefield is the game. In Volatiles you do not just fight in an arena — you lay it out, tile by tile, and the announcement screenshots show exactly how the choice works. Here is what the tile system does, based on the official screenshots (checked October 11, 2026).

## How does tile selection work?

The screenshot labelled **"SELECT A TILE (1 REMAINING)"** shows the core loop of the meta-game:

- You are offered **three candidate tiles**, each shown as a blueprint-style preview of what it contains.
- You place **one** per selection round — "1 remaining" implies you receive a limited number of tile placements per stage, not an unlimited sandbox.
- Each option has a **SELECT** button and a **lock** control, which strongly suggests a reroll/lock economy: lock the tile you want to keep, reroll the rest.

The previews matter because a tile is not just floor: the blueprints show **objects inside** — what look like turrets, machinery, and containers. You are choosing contents, not just geography.

## What do tiles contain?

From the screenshot set, tiles can carry:

- **Defensive objects** — turret-like emplacements and machines, drawn as icons inside the tile preview.
- **Loot and interactables** — containers or chest-like markers. Combined with the HUD objective ("capsules collected", see our [combat breakdown](/mechanics/combat-and-waves/)), some tiles are plainly the fastest route to run objectives.
- **Hazards and modifiers** — the announcement screenshot shows a modifier bar above the arena with entries like a tooltip reading **"Hordes appear 100% more often."** That is a risk/reward dial: harder tile, better payoff.

So the decision space per pick is three-sided: what do I need (loot, defense, space), what can I survive (modifiers), and what do I give up (the two tiles I do not pick).

## Why does building your own arena matter for a roguelike?

Because it converts randomized arena generation — the part of the genre players complain they cannot influence — into a decision. Traditional roguelike levels roll dice; Volatiles hands you the dice. In practice that means:

- **Bad RNG looks different.** You rarely get an unplayable arena; you get a set of tiles where the greedy pick (more hordes, better loot) is available and dangerous.
- **Builds extend to the map.** Turrets and machines mean "what I place" is part of the build, not just what I equip — which is why the Steam tags include Strategy alongside Action.
- **Run planning starts before the first shot.** Pros in any roguelike optimize routes; in Volatiles, the route is something you authored.

## What we still do not know

- **Tile count limits** — how many total tiles an arena can grow to, and whether the "+0" markers beside placed tiles (visible in the screenshot grid) are expansion slots being counted.
- **Whether tile choices are permanent per run** — can you remove or replace a tile later, or is placement final?
- **Modifier range** — "hordes appear 100% more often" is one data point; the modifier pool's full range is unknown.
- **Meta unlocks** — whether new tile types are permanently unlocked through the lab ("new knowledge"), which is the obvious design, but unconfirmed.
